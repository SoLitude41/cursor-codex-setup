## Summary

Justin Welsh critiques how social media incentivizes provocative, oversimplified takes over nuanced discussion. He uses Ryan Petersen's "White Collar Fraud" clip about remote work (which garnered 5.3 million views) to illustrate how removing context and hedging language makes content viral, while a measured exploration of the same topic would barely reach 40,000 viewers. Welsh argues that this engineered certainty often masks the specific circumstances that made something work for one person, leading audiences to mistakenly adopt advice that may not suit their own situations.
